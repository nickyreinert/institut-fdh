Manifest for Informal "Du"

At a time when the internet is increasingly shaped by formal structures, hierarchies, and even walled gardens, this manifesto argues for informal "Du" as an expression of equality, closeness, and community online.

"Du" symbolizes an early internet vision: open exchange beyond traditional social barriers. It stands for mutual recognition and respect independent of age, title, or background.

Return to the Pioneering Spirit

See the internet as it was in the 1990s: a space for enthusiasts and builders where passion mattered more than status.

Reduce Artificial Barriers

Use "Du" to ignore rigid hierarchy: in digital spaces, students and professors can communicate as equals. This encourages healthy skepticism and reduces authority bias.

Preserve Directness

Defend "Du" as a tool that removes unnecessary distance. This was, and still is, a core internet idea.

Against Forced Formality

Reject attempts to transfer rigid old-world formalities into open digital spaces.

Equality

Establish "Du" as a symbol of the internet's democratic premise: everyone is equal.

Respect

"Du" does not imply disrespect. Disrespect can appear in any form of address. Respect is a matter of attitude, not wording.
